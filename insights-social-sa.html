How South Africans Use Social Media — And What It Means for Brands

South Africa has one of the most active online audiences in Africa. Here’s what the platform mix tells us — and how brands can use it to grow.

Social media in South Africa isn’t just entertainment — it’s where people discover brands, compare options, and decide who to trust. By late 2025, tens of millions of South Africans were active on social platforms, and usage continues to grow across age groups.

The platform mix is not “one size fits all”

The way people use platforms often reflects lifestyle and access. Urban audiences tend to be more active on visually-driven and trend-heavy platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, while LinkedIn is strong for professional networking and credibility. These platforms influence culture, purchasing decisions, and brand perception.

Facebook and WhatsApp remain extremely important for reach and direct communication — especially in rural and peri-urban communities where familiarity and accessibility drive usage.

What this means for brands

If your brand relies on only one platform, you’re probably missing part of your audience. The smarter move is to match your message to the role each platform plays:

  • Instagram: Visual presence, brand identity, engagement, and storytelling.
  • LinkedIn: B2B credibility, partnerships, proof of work, and trust-building.
  • Facebook: Broad reach, community connection, announcements, and accessibility.
  • WhatsApp: Direct conversations, enquiries, follow-ups, and customer support.

Our approach at Media On Africa

We help brands choose platforms on purpose — not on hype. For many businesses, Instagram and LinkedIn work as strategic growth channels, while Facebook and WhatsApp keep your reach wide and your communication strong.

The brands that win long-term are the ones that adapt to how people actually use platforms — and build trust consistently through clear messaging and high-quality content.

What People Are Consuming Online — South Africa, North Africa & Global

A premium insights hub built from Deloitte + PwC research and regional digital market data — designed to help brands position smarter in B2B and B2C.

Download Executive Report (PDF)

Executive Summary

Digital media consumption in 2025 is driven by mobile-first behaviour, video dominance, and algorithm-based discovery. PwC’s Africa Entertainment & Media Outlook (2025–2029) positions South Africa as Africa’s most mature entertainment and media market, with digital formats driving the majority of growth. Deloitte’s Digital Media Trends (2025) further confirms that social platforms are now central to media discovery and brand engagement — particularly among younger audiences.

1) South African Content Consumers: What They Consume Most

  • Internet users: 50.8M (≈78.9% penetration)
  • Social media users: 26.7M (≈41.5% of population)
  • Access pattern: Mobile-first (99%+ smartphone access reported in regional datasets)
  • Video signal: PwC reports 76%+ of mobile data consumption is video-driven

Most-consumed content categories (South Africa):

  • Short-form video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)
  • Entrepreneurship & side-hustle content
  • Financial literacy and wealth education
  • Politics, culture, and social discourse
  • Local creator storytelling & entertainment

Strategic implication: South Africa is aspirational and opportunity-driven. Brands win by combining video-first creative with education-led messaging that builds trust and conversion.

2) North African Content Consumers (Examples: Egypt & Morocco)

  • Egypt: 96.3M internet users (81.9%), 50.7M social media user identities (43.1%)
  • Morocco: 35.3M internet users (92.2%), 21.3M social media user identities (55.5%)

Most-consumed content categories (North Africa):

  • Long-form educational video (YouTube-heavy consumption)
  • Culturally aligned & faith-based content
  • Academic and career advancement material
  • Technology tutorials & skill training
  • Regional news and analysis

Strategic implication: North African markets show stronger tolerance for structured, information-heavy content. Educational positioning and enterprise enablement messaging tend to perform well.

3) Global (US, UK, EU) Digital Consumers

  • Industry scale: PwC projects global E&M revenue to exceed $3.5T by 2029
  • Advertising shift: Digital advertising share rises toward ~80% by 2029 in PwC outlook summaries
  • Attention: Deloitte highlights intense competition for consumer attention across platforms

Most-consumed content categories (Global):

  • AI and emerging technology content
  • Productivity & performance optimisation
  • Personal branding and creator economy
  • Investment and financial growth content
  • Niche community ecosystems

Strategic implication: Global markets are ROI-driven and analytics-focused. Enterprise buyers prioritise efficiency, risk reduction, and measurable performance outcomes.

B2B vs B2C: How Brands Should Position

B2C brands win with relatability, frequency, and short-form video that turns value into lifestyle narratives. B2B brands win with authority: research-backed content, case studies, proof-of-work, and clear performance outcomes.

Enterprise & Gamification Insight

Modern organisations increasingly use engagement frameworks (including gamification) for onboarding, training, and system adoption. This shifts “social tech” from a marketing channel to behavioural infrastructure — where content is used to educate, reinforce, and measure adoption.
